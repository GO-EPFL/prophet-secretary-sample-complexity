# Sharp Sample Complexity for the Prophet-Secretary Problem

## Navigation and scope

The public import is `import ProphetSecretary`. The eight subject imports in the [README](../README.md) expose the same mathematics by area. Definitions and auxiliary lemmas remain in subject directories; this guide follows the paper's dependency structure rather than the file order.

The tables map stable paper labels to declarations. **Direct** means a declaration states the claim under the indicated representation; **split** means several declarations establish its clauses; **derived** identifies an assembly or specialization through other proved results. Short namespace prefixes are defined in each section. Source links point to actual declaration locations.

The central statements are the unconditional matching bounds in [`ProphetSecretary.Statistics.sharp_sample_complexity_bounds`](../ProphetSecretary/Statistics/SharpSampleComplexity.lean#L18) and their asymptotic formulation [`ProphetSecretary.Statistics.sharp_sample_complexity`](../ProphetSecretary/Statistics/SharpSampleComplexity.lean#L30). The definitions distinguish a single instance's stopping value from the optimal global competitive ratio, expected guarantees from hard sampling budgets, and fixed-policy derivatives from derivatives of the optimized value.

### Conventions and proof methods

Body convergence uses nonnegative tail levels `0≤x<a`, as in `def:body-convergence`.

The continuation-value threshold rule has deletion monotonicity for every nonnegative terminal reserve. At positive weights it defines a measurable admissible policy on its own instance. Evaluation under another law requires admissibility under that law. The optimizer-attainment statement `lem:canonical-limit` concerns zero-reserve instances.

The numerical condition used by nondegeneracy is `C_PS<√3−1`. Lean proves it internally through `C_PS≤0.73`; the paper obtains it from the stronger external bound `C_PS≤0.7254`. The required numerical input is therefore proved without an external numerical premise.

Law curvature is proved along prescribed likelihood directions. The scalar adaptation-kernel identity corresponds to the directional quadratic form in `upfish:boundarymatrix`. The ambient multivariate matrix and exact trace identities in `upfish:trace` are not separate Lean declarations; the directional proof establishes the required curvature bound.

## Finite stopping model and shared structural results

All linked declaration names below have prefix `ProphetSecretary.Model.`. **Direct** means the paper statement after unfolding model definitions; **derived** indicates a specialization or additional bridge. Limiting-instance extensions are described separately.

### Model conventions

- [Instance](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Instance.lean#L12) gives finite independent nonnegative laws on `ℝ`, without boundedness or finite-support restrictions. [Instance.worldLaw](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Instance.lean#L45) makes the complete offer vector, independent uniform clocks on `[0,1]`, and the auxiliary random seed independent.
- [Instance.expectedMaximum](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Instance.lean#L129), `Instance.totalFirstMoment`, `Instance.tailCount`, and `Instance.positiveCount` represent `M,S,L_b,L`, with empty maximum zero and strict tail exceedances. [Instance.HasPositiveFiniteMaximum](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Instance.lean#L214) requires an integrable maximum and positive mean. Auxiliary laws may have mean zero. [Instance.integrable_maximum_iff](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Instance.lean#L116) identifies finite-maximum integrability with integrability of every marginal.
- [Policy](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Policy.lean#L21) reads current identity/time/value, seed, and full strict-past history, including zero identities and timestamps. [RandomizedPolicy](../ProphetSecretary/Model/ScalarObjectives.lean#L18) adds joint measurability and an independent probability seed. Execution selects the earliest acceptance; [Policy.ActualSelects](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Outcome.lean#L21) resolves simultaneous acceptances by identity order. No selection gives reward zero and duration one.
- [optimalScalarValue](../ProphetSecretary/Model/ScalarObjectives.lean#L105) takes the supremum over general randomized policies. [optimalCompetitiveRatio](../ProphetSecretary/Model/ScalarObjectives.lean#L109) takes the infimum over all finite sizes and positive finite benchmarks; `scalarGap` and `normalizedScalarGap` use this same constant.
- [Learner](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Learner.lean#L22) is fixed before the unknown law. Requests see only seed and transcript; the executor releases the next unused requested sample. [Learner.hard_budget](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Learner.lean#L54) bounds requests by `k` per distribution on every outcome. Training/seed randomness is independent of online offers/clocks. [Learner.Guarantees](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Learner.lean#L85) averages all randomness over positive finite benchmarks; [FeasibleBudget](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Learner.lean#L91) uses one budget independent of instance size.
- Causal clock/random-order adapters give equivalence: [clock_rank_reward_values_eq](../ProphetSecretary/Model/RankCompetitiveRatio.lean#L23) identifies reward sets; [optimalRankCompetitiveRatio_eq](../ProphetSecretary/Model/RankCompetitiveRatio.lean#L45) identifies competitive constants. [rankFeasibleBudget_iff](../ProphetSecretary/Model/RankBudgetEquivalence.lean#L23) preserves adaptive budgets. Duration uses clocks.

### Continuation and canonical policies

[Instance.continuationValue](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Continuation.lean#L78) is `V(P_A;w,c*u)`, excluding accrued duration and retaining identities. [Instance.valueThreshold](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Continuation.lean#L83) divides child continuation by `w`; the threshold policy accepts ties. [Instance.valueThreshold_monotone](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Continuation.lean#L107) proves monotonicity when unseen options are added. Observed zeros leave the unseen set even when rejected; [Instance.valueThreshold_rejects_zero](../ProphetSecretary/Model/Continuation.lean#L230) proves preterminal rejection for `w,c>0`.

[Instance.valuePolicy_optimal](../ProphetSecretary/Model/BellmanOptimal.lean#L170) proves threshold attainment against the full class for integrable maxima, `w>0,c≥0`; the Bellman equation follows as [Instance.optimalScalarValue_eq_bellmanValue](../ProphetSecretary/Model/BellmanOptimal.lean#L197). [RandomizedPolicy.bellman_performanceDifference](../ProphetSecretary/Model/BellmanPerformance.lean#L175) identifies actual regret with expected reached Bellman losses.

The quotient rule requires positive reward weight. For normalized endpoint weights, use [Instance.unitCanonicalPolicy](../ProphetSecretary/Model/UnitCanonicalPolicy.lean#L15): it waits at `λ=0`. [Instance.unitCanonicalPolicy_optimal](../ProphetSecretary/Model/UnitCanonicalPolicy.lean#L24) covers the entire interval `[0,1]`. For arbitrary `w=0,c≥0`, [optimalScalarValue_zero_rewardWeight](../ProphetSecretary/Model/BellmanBase.lean#L42) gives `V(P;0,c)=c`, attained by waiting.

### Infinite expected maxima (`upouter:infinite`)

Nonnegative extended expectations are represented by [`ProphetSecretary.Model.Instance.extendedExpectedMaximum`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/ExtendedExpectations.lean#L17), [`ProphetSecretary.Model.RandomizedPolicy.extendedExpectedReward`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/ExtendedExpectations.lean#L23), and [`ProphetSecretary.Model.Learner.extendedExpectedReward`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/LearnerMixtureExpectations.lean#L17). These are `ℝ≥0∞` Lebesgue integrals of `ENNReal.ofReal` rewards. Under an integrable maximum they agree with the existing real-valued expectations. The sample-complexity benchmark continues to use positive finite expected maxima.

For n>0, [`ProphetSecretary.Model.RandomizedPolicy.uniformIdentity`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/UniformIdentity.lean#L20) chooses an identity with probability 1/n independently of offers and clocks, then accepts only that identity. Its extended reward is the average of the marginal extended means. The maximum is bounded by their finite sum, so an infinite expected maximum gives infinite reward for this one fixed uniform strategy: [`ProphetSecretary.Model.RandomizedPolicy.uniformIdentity_extendedExpectedReward_eq_top`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/UniformIdentity.lean#L145).

[`ProphetSecretary.Model.Learner.exists_infiniteMeanRepair_any_size`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/InfiniteMeanGuarantees.lean#L116) supplies one instance-independent learner for each original learner and k≥1. It chooses the uniform strategy with probability 1/k before training. Every raw outcome retains the original per-distribution hard budget; the uniform branch requests no samples. Its extended reward is infinite when the maximum has infinite expectation, and its finite-mean reward loss is at most M/k, including M=0. The empty instance is included without selecting a nonexistent identity.

At k=1, [`ProphetSecretary.Model.Learner.exists_budgetOne_noTraining_repair_any_size`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/InfiniteMeanGuarantees.lean#L135) gives a learner making zero requests on every raw seed and sample array. The independent-mixture expectation identity is [`ProphetSecretary.Model.Learner.mix_extendedExpectedReward`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/LearnerMixtureExpectations.lean#L59); its finite counterpart and the exact deficit are [`ProphetSecretary.Model.Learner.infiniteMeanRepair_expectedReward`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/InfiniteMeanGuarantees.lean#L12) and [`ProphetSecretary.Model.Learner.infiniteMeanRepair_deficit`](../ProphetSecretary/Model/InfiniteMeanGuarantees.lean#L25).


## Lower bound and limiting instances

The lower bound uses actual finite product laws and adaptive training transcripts. `FeasibleBudget ε k` means that one learner for each finite dimension guarantees `(C−ε)M` on every positive finite benchmark, using the same hard budget k per distribution. Expected reward averages training and online randomness. The final lower and sharp-complexity theorems have no additional experiment, compactness or numerical premises.

The limiting representation retains countably many independent individual variables, including their observed zero identities, and an independent marked Poisson process with locally finite tails above a terminal reserve. Scalar value is the supremum over measurable causal randomized policies whose first accepted occurrence exists almost surely. The canonical Markov optimizer, its admissibility and its optimality are proved separately from these definitions.

Body convergence takes the original-sequence limit at each fixed retained prefix first, then sends the prefix to infinity. Residual rarity is eventual in the sequence after the prefix is chosen. The below-reserve clause uses **0≤x<a**. Maxima and scalar-value convergence are theorem conclusions, rather than fields of the convergence structure. Scalar convergence is uniform on compact sets of nonnegative weights.

For direct approximations, retained prefixes, cutoffs and numbers of virtual variables are chosen from the base law before θ varies. Each perturbed law uses that same schedule. Reward normalization divides by the **base** expected maximum M_m, with weights `wM_m/(wM_m+c)` and `c/(wM_m+c)` kept fixed through the perturbation. Likelihood scores refer to the complete offer vector or complete Poisson configuration, including future occurrences, solely as multipliers inside expectations; policies still receive only current and past observations.

The envelope differentiates the optimized scalar value and identifies its derivative with the fixed base canonical policy's score expectation. The duration and target-response paths themselves hold that policy fixed. Uniform finite controls use a common cap, interior weights, a positive-level tail profile and bounded total score information; they do not assume a uniform bound on the sum of offer means. Perturbed direct-value convergence is stated for each fixed θ. The score-transfer limits and regret modulus are separate results.

“Direct” identifies an endpoint stating the paper claim; “split” covers a compound claim with several endpoints; “derived” records a construction or consequence assembled from the linked results. Links below are relative to `docs/Formalization.md`.

## Verification

From the repository root, run the commands in the [README](../README.md). `lake build` checks the mathematical sources using the pinned toolchain and dependency lockfile. The GitHub workflow uses the same build and axiom-check commands; its status is separate from a local run.

[`scripts/CheckAxioms.lean`](../scripts/CheckAxioms.lean) imports the public module, checks that it reaches every mathematical source file, and audits every declaration originating in those modules, including private/generated declarations and declarations in other namespaces. Lean's transitive axiom collector follows types and proof bodies. The fixed allowlist is `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`; the process exits unsuccessfully on missing imports, disallowed axioms or import errors. Additional command-line module names add audit targets without replacing the public import or changing the allowlist.

This foundational audit does not check manuscript correspondence. The statement-level distinctions, representations and qualifications above describe that separate specification boundary. Conditional general-purpose lemmas are identified by their hypotheses; the final upper bound, constructed critical experiment, lower bound and sharp-complexity statements discharge their required premises.
